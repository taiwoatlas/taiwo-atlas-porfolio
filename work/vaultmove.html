VaultMove

A moving-services concept built to remove the guesswork from relocation — a six-step move builder, live tracking from pickup to delivery, and a dedicated path for business relocations.

Context

VaultMove is a concept site for a residential, commercial and long-distance moving company, organized around a single idea: nothing about a move should be a surprise. A six-step move builder and a live-feeling tracking view carry a visitor from a first estimate through to delivery.

The Challenge

Moving is stressful largely because of uncertainty — what will it cost, who's coming, and where are my things right now. The challenge was designing a flow that answers all three without turning into an overwhelming multi-page form.

Objective

Replace moving-day uncertainty with a visible sequence of status states, from quote to delivery.

Approach

I built a six-step 'Build My Move' flow — move type, home details, inventory by room, optional services, a live estimate, and booking — plus a separate move-tracking view showing pickup, transit and delivery stages for a sample long-distance move. A dedicated business-relocation section covers office and commercial moves separately from residential.

Design Direction

A confident, premium dark theme punctuated by VaultMove's branded red, leaning on real crew and moving-day photography so the site feels like a functioning logistics company rather than a generic mover template.

Technical Implementation

Built with semantic HTML5, modular CSS and vanilla JavaScript. The move builder, cost estimator and tracking view are client-side simulations against demo data — clearly labeled as illustrative rather than connected to a live dispatch or GPS system.

HTML5 CSS3 JavaScript

Outcome

A moving-services concept that replaces a phone-quote-driven process with a transparent, step-by-step builder and live tracking view, while keeping all pricing and tracking data clearly framed as a demonstration.

Lesson

Service anxiety in moving and logistics is mostly about not knowing what happens next — a visible status sequence solves more of the problem than reassuring copy does.
