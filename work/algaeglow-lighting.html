AlgaeGlow Lighting

A conceptual bioluminescence-inspired outdoor lighting platform for municipalities, parks and public spaces — pairing design storytelling with a fixture configurator and a lighting comparison tool.

Context

AlgaeGlow Lighting imagines an outdoor-lighting company whose design language borrows from bioluminescence — the idea that public lighting could feel less like glaring overhead fixtures and more like a softer, ground-directed glow. The site is upfront that this is design thinking, not measured commercial lighting performance.

The Challenge

A concept built on a biological metaphor risks feeling more like poetry than a product. The challenge was giving "lighting inspired by life" something concrete to stand on — a real day/night interaction and a configurable fixture, not just mood language.

Objective

Explain a genuinely novel material — bioluminescent algae panels — clearly enough that a first-time visitor understands the mechanism, not just the mood.

Approach

I anchored the homepage on a drag-to-reveal day/night slider so the core idea (a softer, ground-directed glow versus harsh overhead glare) is something a visitor experiences immediately, then backed it with a municipal catalog and a "Glow Lab" configurator (lamp style, height, intensity, environment) to make the concept explorable.

Design Direction

A deep, nocturnal palette with a warm bioluminescent accent color, letting the lighting concept itself provide the contrast. Comparison sliders (traditional lighting vs. the ambient-glow concept) do more communicative work than descriptive copy alone.

Technical Implementation

Built with semantic HTML5, modular CSS and vanilla JavaScript. Every specification, fixture figure and street-simulation value on the site is explicitly labeled illustrative — there's no live lux measurement or commercial product data behind it.

HTML5 CSS3 JavaScript

Outcome

A sustainability-minded lighting concept that uses direct visual comparison — not just descriptive claims — to make an unfamiliar design idea legible, while being consistently clear that its figures are illustrative rather than measured performance data.

Lesson

Environmental technology doesn't need leaf icons to feel credible — the material and the mechanism, shown plainly, do more convincing.
